    What's inside

    Built for people who listen properly

    Every one of these ships in the current build — no waitlists, no paid tier, no account.

    Real offline downloads

    Keep tracks, albums and whole playlists on the device. No connection, no degradation, no silent expiry.

    A feed that learns

    Home is ranked from what you actually played — recent plays, likes and followed artists — not a shuffled list of whatever is trending.

    Share playlists by QR

    A playlist becomes a code another phone can scan from across the table. The whole thing travels in the code — no server, no account, no link rot.

    Ambient mode

    Dock it and the screen becomes an aurora lit by the album art, with a big clock and slow drift that protects OLED panels overnight.

    Lyrics that keep time

    Synced, line-by-line lyrics that scroll with the track, and a plain view for everything else.

    Sleep timer and EQ

    Fade out at the end of the track rather than mid-chorus, and shape the sound with a built-in equaliser and crossfade.

    Wear OS companion

    Skip, pause and browse from the watch, with its own screens rather than a stretched phone layout.

    Make it yours

    Light, dark and a true-black AMOLED theme, Material You colour from your wallpaper, or pick an accent yourself.

    Getting it on your phone

    Three steps, about a minute

    MusicSM isn't on the Play Store, so Android will ask permission before installing it. That prompt is normal for any app installed outside the store.

    Download the APK

    Tap the download button above. Your browser may warn that this file type can harm your device — that warning appears for every APK.

    Allow your browser to install

    Open the downloaded file. Android will offer to take you to Install unknown apps; enable it for the browser you used, then come back.

    Install and open

    Tap Install, then Open. Nothing to sign up for — the app works the moment it starts.

    Requires Android 7.0 or newer. Downloads are served straight from the project's GitHub releases, so you can always verify the file at source.
